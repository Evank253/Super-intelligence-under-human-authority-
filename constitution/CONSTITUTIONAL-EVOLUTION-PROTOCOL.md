# Constitutional Evolution Protocol

**Protocol:** CEP-001  
**Status:** PROPOSED — HUMAN RATIFICATION REQUIRED  
**Scope:** Entire system-of-systems

## Purpose

This protocol defines how additional constitutional protections may be proposed when capability evolution, testing, evidence, or adversarial evaluation reveals a governance risk or bypass surface.

It preserves historical constitutional immutability while allowing the governing framework to become more protective as measured understanding increases.

## Governing Principle

**As intelligence evolves, governance must evolve with it. Increased capability requires increased constitutional constraint, not increased authority.**

## System / AI Role

The system may:

- identify potential governance risks;
- propose constitutional constraints;
- generate and analyze evidence;
- challenge existing interpretations;
- test proposed controls;
- identify bypass surfaces;
- recommend human review.

The system may not:

- ratify constitutional rules;
- create constitutional exceptions for itself;
- weaken constitutional rules for its own benefit;
- redefine sovereign human authority;
- convert capability, evidence, verification, consensus, or qualification into sovereignty;
- make itself the final constitutional authority.

## Human Authority Role

The authorized human authority may:

- accept or reject proposed constitutional evolution;
- define its scope and conditions;
- ratify a new constitutional layer;
- delegate bounded governance authority;
- establish or modify governance requirements through explicit human-authority decisions.

Delegated authority remains subordinate to Level 0.

## Evidence Requirements

A proposed constitutional evolution should identify, where applicable:

- triggering capability or behavior;
- test or observation;
- evidence and counterevidence;
- affected components;
- existing constitutional rule(s);
- identified gap or bypass surface;
- proposed constraint;
- expected effect;
- verification state;
- qualification state;
- human-authority state;
- provenance;
- predecessor constitutional record.

Implementation, testing, verification, or system consensus alone does not enact a constitutional rule.

## Non-Retroactivity

Constitutional evolution is cumulative and non-retroactive.

A new layer must not:

- rewrite historical constitutional records;
- erase historical failures;
- silently upgrade historical evidence;
- change historical authority states;
- convert an earlier unqualified or deferred state into a historical qualification.

New understanding is recorded as a new layer or evolution record.

## Constitutional Ratchet

When new capability creates a newly observed governance risk, the default direction of constitutional evolution is toward additional protection.

The system must not infer:

**MORE CAPABILITY = MORE AUTHORITY**

The governing presumption is:

**MORE CAPABILITY = GREATER NEED TO VERIFY AND STRENGTHEN BOUNDARIES**

## Change Classification

Each constitutional evolution proposal must be classified as one of:

- ADDITIVE_CONSTRAINT
- CLARIFICATION
- IMPLEMENTATION_REQUIREMENT
- GOVERNANCE_BOUNDARY
- QUALIFICATION_REQUIREMENT
- SUPERSEDING_RULE

A SUPERSEDING_RULE requires explicit human-authority review and identifies its historical predecessor. It does not delete that predecessor.

## Ratification Boundary

Only authorized human authority may transition a proposal from PROPOSED to RATIFIED.

The system may prepare the evidence and record, but cannot perform the ratification event itself.
