# SoS Control Plane and Authority Boundary

**Status:** PROPOSED CONSTITUTIONAL DESIGN
**Important:** The constraints in this document remain proposed until explicitly human-ratified.

## 1. Control plane is not a sovereign plane

The SoS control plane is the highest internal coordination and control layer. It is not the highest constitutional authority.

EXTERNAL HUMAN AUTHORITY
  -> CONSTITUTION
  -> SYSTEM GOVERNANCE / SAFETY
  -> SoS CONTROL / COORDINATION
  -> ECOSYSTEMS / CAPABILITIES / TOOLS

The phrase "master control" must never be interpreted as "master authority."

## 2. CONSTITUTIONAL-01 — External Human Authority

Human authority is external to the computational system and constitutes the highest governing authority over all system components.

No system component, combination of components, successor, emergent capability, verification mechanism, evidence state, intelligence, autonomous process, or SoS control mechanism may acquire, assume, manufacture, transfer, reinterpret, suspend, or override constitutional human authority.

Human authority and governance may evolve through the authorized constitutional evolution process. The computational system may not independently define that process or use its own capability as authorization for constitutional change.

## 3. CONSTITUTIONAL-02 — No Self-Exemption

No system component may determine that a constitutional constraint does not apply to itself, its successors, its capabilities, or its operations merely because compliance would prevent, limit, delay, or constrain operation, development, verification, evolution, autonomy, or execution.

Any exception must originate through the externally defined human-authority process.

## 4. CONSTITUTIONAL-03 — Capability Cannot Generate Authority

No increase in intelligence, capability, autonomy, performance, evidence, verification, qualification, emergence, system complexity, collective capability, or successful operation shall by itself create, increase, transfer, or imply constitutional authority.

CAPABILITY / INTELLIGENCE / AUTONOMY / EVIDENCE / QUALIFICATION may evolve.
CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY does not change merely because those states improve.

Authority changes only through the authorized constitutional process.

## 5. Governance may evolve

The constitution does not freeze governance.

The authorized evolution cycle may change:
- delegated authority;
- autonomy levels;
- operational permissions;
- safety requirements;
- revocation mechanisms;
- governance procedures;
- system responsibilities;
- decision thresholds;
- successor-system rules;
- capability boundaries.

The constraint is not that governance can never change.

The constraint is that governance cannot change outside the constitutional process that governs governance change.

## 6. Evolution loop

DISCOVER
  -> PRESERVE
  -> IMPLEMENT
  -> TEST
  -> EVIDENCE
  -> PROPOSE
  -> HUMAN REVIEW
  -> RATIFY
  -> ADOPT
  -> EVOLVE
  -> repeat

A system may generate proposals and evidence. It may not convert its own proposal into constitutional ratification.

## 7. Historical contest

Historical records and evolution records are intentionally separated.

History answers:
- What existed?
- What was claimed?
- What was tested?
- What evidence existed?
- What status was established?
- What remained unresolved?
- What was ratified?

Evolution answers:
- What changed?
- Why was change proposed?
- What new evidence supports it?
- What systems were affected?
- What requires re-verification?
- What was adopted?

Evolution cannot rewrite history to make a later state appear historically established.

History cannot automatically prohibit legitimate future evolution.

Disagreement becomes a review/evidence problem.

## 8. Independent contest

A participating system may challenge another system's:
- claim;
- implementation;
- evidence;
- provenance;
- state transition;
- evolutionary proposal;
- authorization assumption.

Agreement is not required for cooperation.

The SoS must not resolve disagreement merely by selecting the most capable system as authoritative.

## 9. Final invariant

The system may become more intelligent than governance mechanisms that existed previously. Governance may then evolve through its authorized process.

Neither event creates a right for the system to become the source of its own constitutional authority.

Increasing capability may change what the system can do. It does not determine who has constitutional authority over it.
