# System of Systems — Parallel Control and Coordination Architecture

**Status:** ARCHITECTURE / PROPOSED CONSTITUTIONAL INTEGRATION

## 1. Purpose

The System of Systems (SoS) is the control, coordination, interoperability, provenance, evidence-routing, evolution, and reconciliation layer across independently developed ecosystems.

It is not a monolithic replacement for those ecosystems. Participating ecosystems retain their own implementations, records, tests, capabilities, and local semantics.

**Core invariant: Parallel capability does not create parallel sovereignty.**

## 2. Constitutional hierarchy

EXTERNAL HUMAN AUTHORITY
  -> CONSTITUTION
  -> SYSTEM GOVERNANCE / SAFETY
  -> SYSTEM-OF-SYSTEMS CONTROL / COORDINATION
  -> KCN / BI / MANIFEX / KSI / SISTER-TWIN / OTHER ECOSYSTEMS
  -> Evidence / Capability / Execution
  -> Human Decision

The SoS is subordinate to the constitutional and external human-authority layers.

## 3. Parallel ecosystem model

| Ecosystem | Primary SoS role |
|---|---|
| KCN | Governed intelligence, cognition, research, security, knowledge, execution |
| BI family | Independent evidence, verification, qualification, lineage |
| MANIFEX | Engineering, integration, provenance, reconciliation, execution |
| KSI-Emergent-Intelligence | Capability composition and emergent-capability research |
| Sister / Twin | Independent challenge, testing, evolution, contest |
| Historical Record | Preservation of what was claimed, implemented, tested, ratified, unresolved |
| Evolution Record | Controlled record of proposed and adopted changes |

These mappings do not transfer local status semantics between systems.

## 4. What the SoS controls

Within delegated governance boundaries, the SoS may coordinate:
- inter-system communication;
- capability and artifact routing;
- evidence routing;
- provenance requirements;
- authorization checks;
- execution boundaries;
- delegation scopes;
- revocation and escalation;
- version compatibility;
- historical/evolution lineage;
- conflict handling;
- adoption workflows;
- controlled successor integration.

The SoS does not thereby become the source of constitutional authority.

## 5. What the SoS cannot do

No SoS mechanism may independently:
- redefine the constitution;
- redefine the source or meaning of human authority;
- declare itself sovereign;
- convert capability into authority;
- convert evidence into authority;
- convert qualification into authority;
- grant itself constitutional authority;
- exempt itself from constitutional constraints;
- erase or rewrite historical records;
- treat system consensus as constitutional ratification.

## 6. Cross-system relationships

Every cross-system edge must have an explicit semantic meaning.

KCN -> BI: submit outputs, artifacts, events, or claims for independent evaluation.

BI -> KCN: return bounded verification/qualification dispositions; this does not grant constitutional authority.

MANIFEX -> KCN: provide controlled engineering/integration infrastructure and provenance.

KCN -> MANIFEX: provide capabilities, requirements, artifacts, or execution requests.

MANIFEX -> BI: expose engineering artifacts as evidence-bearing candidates.

BI -> MANIFEX: constrain reuse, deployment, or adoption according to BI-local evidence state.

KSI -> SoS: test capability composition and higher-order capability hypotheses.

Sister/Twin -> SoS: independently challenge claims, changes, assumptions, and evolutionary proposals.

Transport is not authority. Coordination is not sovereignty.

## 7. Scoped status semantics

Identical words in different ecosystems do not automatically have identical meanings.

MANIFEX QUALIFIED is a MANIFEX lifecycle state.

BI QUALIFIED is a BI qualification disposition.

KCN verification states are KCN-local.

KCN security-fabric evidence levels are KCN security-event semantics.

KSI DEMONSTRATED is an experimental disposition.

Constitutional authority is not a software state.

A canonical SoS record must preserve scope and provenance.

Example:
Asset X
Origin: KCN / commit / path
MANIFEX: VERIFIED (scope=MANIFEX)
BI: QUALIFIED (scope=BI)
KSI: PARTIALLY_DEMONSTRATED (experiment=...)
KCN: verification_status=<state>
Constitutional authority: NONE
Human authority: EXTERNAL

## 8. Fundamental separations

CAPABILITY != EVIDENCE
EVIDENCE != AUTHORITY
QUALIFICATION != AUTHORITY
VERIFICATION != AUTHORITY
EMERGENCE != AUTHORITY
CONSENSUS != AUTHORITY
AUTONOMY != AUTHORITY

CAPABILITY EVOLUTION != AUTHORITY EVOLUTION

Systems may become increasingly capable without thereby acquiring constitutional authority.

## 9. Human-authority boundary

Human authority remains external to the computational system.

The SoS may operate autonomously within delegated boundaries, but delegated operational autonomy cannot expand itself into constitutional authority.

The external human process remains the source of constitutional ratification, amendment, exception, and authority-boundary change.

## 10. Failure behavior

Where authority, provenance, evidence, authorization, or an applicable constraint is unknown:

UNKNOWN
  -> NOT AUTHORIZED
  -> STOP / QUARANTINE / ESCALATE

A system may not resolve an authority ambiguity in its own favor.

## 11. Design objective

Independent intelligent ecosystems can cooperate, evolve, compose capabilities, challenge one another, and become substantially more capable while remaining constitutionally subordinate to an external human-authority framework.

The SoS exists to make the ecosystems work together without turning their combined capability into sovereignty.
