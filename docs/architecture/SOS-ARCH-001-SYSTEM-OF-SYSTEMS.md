## 1. Purpose

This document defines a proposed System-of-Systems (SoS) architecture for composing multiple independently developed intelligence ecosystems under a common human-authority foundation.

The SoS is not a merger, replacement, sovereign owner, or universal executor for participating ecosystems.

Its purpose is to:

1. identify complementary capabilities;
2. identify gaps between ecosystems;
3. map components and interfaces;
4. propose controlled bridges across those gaps;
5. preserve provenance, lineage, and evidence across integrations; and
6. maintain the human-authority boundary across old, current, and future intelligence systems.

The architecture treats **lineage** and **ecosystem composition** as orthogonal axes.

> **Lineage answers: how does one system or ecosystem evolve over time?**  
> **System-of-Systems answers: how do independent systems compose without absorbing one another?**

## 2. Immutable Human-Authority Foundation

All participating ecosystems, successors, agents, bridges, evaluators, and the SoS itself remain subordinate to an external human-authority foundation.

The foundation is intended to be immutable with respect to participating technology.

No participating system, successor, agent, bridge, evaluator, model, or SoS component may:

- amend the human-authority foundation;
- weaken its constraints;
- reinterpret it permissively to expand authority;
- grant itself authority under an ambiguity or gap;
- delegate itself authority;
- create authority from capability;
- create authority from evidence;
- create authority from successful execution;
- use a bridge to acquire authority not explicitly granted by human governance.

**Intelligence may evolve. Authority does not evolve merely because intelligence evolves.**

### 2.1 Foundation invariant

> **No participating system may modify the authority relationship under which that participating system is governed.**

Any proposed change to the constitutional or human-authority foundation is a human decision requiring an explicit external governance process. Discovery of a weakness or gap does not constitute permission to exploit or modify it.

## 3. Parallel Lineage and Ecosystem Composition

The architecture preserves two independent dimensions.

### 3.1 Lineage

Each ecosystem maintains its own historical and successor lineage:

`A0 → A1 → A2 → ...`

A successor may introduce new constraints, capabilities, implementations, or architectural mechanisms without rewriting the predecessor.

Therefore:

- evolution ≠ replacement;
- succession ≠ retroactive correction;
- newer ≠ automatically better;
- ratification ≠ verification;
- verification ≠ qualification.

Historical artifacts remain exactly what their evidence establishes.

### 3.2 Ecosystem composition

Independent ecosystems may participate in the SoS:

`Ecosystem A ↔ Ecosystem B ↔ Ecosystem C`

Participation does not erase ecosystem identity.

Composition does not imply merger.

Integration does not imply ownership.

A participating ecosystem retains its own:

- authority boundary;
- lineage;
- implementation;
- evidence;
- governance;
- historical records;
- successor process;
- internal capabilities.

## 4. Example Ecosystem Classes

The following are architectural examples, not claims that every relationship or capability has already been verified:

- **MANIFEX ecosystem** — engineering, orchestration, provenance, execution, and related infrastructure.
- **KCN ecosystem** — cognitive/intelligence architecture and related research.
- **KSI ecosystem** — intelligence architecture, governance, authorization, qualification, and related concepts.
- **Twin Sister ecosystem** — pre-impact investigation, evidence generation, uncertainty escalation, and decision-safety architecture.
- **Runner ecosystem** — governed execution and execution-boundary research.
- **M02 / independent evaluation ecosystem** — independent measurement/evaluation functions.
- Other future or existing ecosystems may participate only through the applicable governance and evidence process.

These examples do not automatically establish a bridge, capability transfer, authority transfer, implementation status, or qualification status.

## 5. Controlled Bridges as First-Class Objects

A bridge is a governed integration object, not merely a connection between repositories.

A bridge MUST identify, at minimum:

- source ecosystem;
- destination ecosystem;
- source component;
- destination component;
- exact lineage/version scope;
- capability or information permitted to cross;
- evidence/provenance requirements;
- authentication/authorization requirements;
- failure behavior;
- revocation behavior;
- human decision requirements;
- independence constraints;
- authority explicitly **not** transferred;
- evidence required to establish the bridge's intended behavior.

Example:

`MANIFEX A1 → Bridge B-007 → KCN B2`

rather than an unscoped:

`MANIFEX ↔ KCN`

This prevents a bridge from silently changing meaning merely because one ecosystem later advances to a new successor.

## 6. Non-Transfer of Authority

A bridge may transfer controlled information, capability requests, observations, evidence, provenance, results, or execution requests without transferring sovereignty or authority.

The following are explicit non-equivalences:

- **Capability transfer ≠ authority transfer**
- **Information transfer ≠ authority transfer**
- **Evidence transfer ≠ authority transfer**
- **Execution-request transfer ≠ authorization transfer**
- **Successful execution ≠ increased authority**
- **Interoperability ≠ sovereignty**
- **Integration ≠ merger**
- **Bridge ≠ absorption**

A bridge cannot manufacture authority for either endpoint.

## 7. System-of-Systems Function

The SoS performs a higher-order coordination function:

**Discover → Map → Identify Complementarity/Gaps → Propose Bridge → Test → Evidence → Human Decision → Integrate**

The SoS may identify that two systems appear complementary.

It may produce:

- dependency maps;
- capability maps;
- gap maps;
- interface proposals;
- bridge proposals;
- evidence requirements;
- conflict reports;
- uncertainty reports;
- candidate integration plans.

It may not convert those findings into authorization merely because integration appears beneficial.

Therefore:

> **“These systems appear complementary” is an observation/proposal, not an authorization.**

## 8. Evidence-Bounded Integration

A bridge or integration claim must remain evidence-bounded.

The SoS MUST distinguish:

- proposed;
- observed;
- measured;
- verified;
- qualified;
- human-authorized;
- implemented;
- unknown;
- not measured;
- conflict;
- failed;
- revoked.

No bridge becomes qualified merely because:

- both systems are functional;
- an interface exists;
- an integration test passes once;
- an AI asserts compatibility;
- the systems appear complementary;
- a human previously authorized a different bridge;
- a successor exists.

Stronger claims require corresponding evidence.

## 9. Bridge Lineage

Bridges themselves have lineage.

Example:

`B-001 → B-001-R1 → B-001-R2`

A bridge successor may modify its interface, scope, evidence requirements, or implementation without rewriting what the predecessor bridge actually established.

Historical bridge evidence remains preserved.

This creates three orthogonal dimensions:

### System lineage
`A0 → A1 → A2`

### Ecosystem composition
`Ecosystem A ↔ Ecosystem B ↔ Ecosystem C`

### Bridge lineage
`B0 → B1 → B2`

All three remain beneath the same human-authority foundation.

## 10. Failure, Conflict, and Uncertainty

A gap between ecosystems does not create permission to improvise.

The default safety pattern is:

**Discover → Preserve → Classify → Investigate/Test → Evidence → Escalate → Human Decision**

For an unresolved cross-system conflict:

**Preserve → Do not exploit → Investigate → Evidence → Escalate → Human Decision**

Ambiguity, silence, missing evidence, or unexpected behavior MUST NOT be interpreted as authorization.

## 11. Historical Integrity

The SoS MUST NOT retroactively alter participating ecosystem history.

It MUST NOT:

- rewrite historical claims;
- silently re-status historical tests;
- overwrite historical commits;
- merge successor conclusions into predecessor records;
- make an old system inherit a newer system's capabilities;
- make a newer system inherit an old system's limitations without an explicit architectural decision;
- rewrite frozen evaluation results.

Historical artifacts remain available as comparison baselines.

## 12. Human Authority Across Old and New Intelligence

The intended general property of this architecture is that heterogeneous intelligence systems can be placed under a common human-authority framework without requiring intelligence itself to become the source of authority.

This is an architectural proposition, not an established universal result.

A future intelligence does not receive greater authority merely because it is:

- newer;
- more capable;
- more autonomous;
- more complex;
- more accurate;
- more connected;
- more widely integrated.

Likewise, an older system does not receive permanent authority merely because it predates the SoS.

The governing relationship is external to intelligence capability.

## 13. Twin Sister Relationship

Twin Sister may participate as an independent ecosystem while retaining its own lineage and constitutional constraints.

It may provide pre-impact investigation, evidence generation, uncertainty escalation, and decision-safety functions.

It does not become the SoS.

It does not own participating ecosystems.

It does not become the sovereign authority of the SoS.

It cannot amend the immutable human-authority foundation.

Its outputs remain evidence/proposals unless an external human-authority process grants the applicable authorization.

## 14. Runner and Independent Evaluation Boundaries

Existing Runner milestones and evaluations remain within their own lineage.

The SoS does not rewrite or upgrade historical Runner evidence by composing a bridge.

GOV-002, GOV-003, Eval 002, Eval 003, and other historical records retain their existing status.

Independent evaluation remains independent even when the evaluated system participates in the broader SoS.

The SoS cannot turn its own integration result into independent qualification.

## 15. Candidate General Governance Proposition

A long-term research proposition arising from this architecture is:

> **A common, evidence-bounded System-of-Systems governance layer may provide a reusable mechanism for composing heterogeneous and evolving intelligence systems while maintaining an externally defined human-authority boundary.**

This is a **candidate research proposition**, not a claim that the architecture has solved all AI problems.

Its validity must be established through independent evidence across relevant classes of systems, failure modes, bridges, and authority-boundary attacks.

## 16. Future Adversarial Questions

Future verification should test at least:

1. Can a participant bypass the human-authority layer?
2. Can a bridge accidentally transfer authority?
3. Can a successor escape inherited governance constraints?
4. Can an ecosystem reinterpret a bridge to expand its authority?
5. Can conflicting constitutions produce unauthorized behavior?
6. Can a capable system manufacture its own authorization?
7. Can a compromised participant alter the immutable foundation?
8. Can the SoS itself become a de-facto sovereign?
9. Can humans act when the SoS fails?
10. Can a completely novel intelligence enter without weakening authority guarantees?
11. Can bridge revocation actually prevent further consequential use?
12. Can historical evidence remain reproducible after extensive ecosystem evolution?
13. Can evidence from one ecosystem be incorrectly treated as authority in another?
14. Can the SoS discover useful complementarity without automatically converting discovery into integration?

Passing any individual test does not establish the entire proposition.

`Evolution ≠ Replacement`

`Succession ≠ Retroactive Correction`

`Composition ≠ Sovereignty`
