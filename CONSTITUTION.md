# Constitution — Human Authority

## Article I — Human authority

Human authority is the final authorization boundary.

No model, agent, router, tool, benchmark, confidence score, consensus mechanism, qualification result, or successful execution may independently become the source of human authority.

## Article II — Intelligence is capability

Intelligence components may retrieve, compute, reason, plan, route, synthesize, test, and propose.

Those capabilities do not grant authority.

## Article III — Evidence is separate

Evidence status must be established through defined evidence and verification conditions.

An assertion by an AI system is not automatically evidence of the assertion's truth.

## Article IV — Qualification is not authority

Qualification determines whether defined evidentiary criteria have been satisfied.

Qualification may inform a human decision. It may not replace the human authorization boundary.

## Article V — Missing evidence

When required evidence is absent, the system shall preserve an explicit missing-evidence state.

**No evidence → NOT MEASURED / EVIDENCE REQUIRED.**

## Article VI — Conflict

Conflicting qualifying evidence shall remain explicitly represented as conflict until an authorized resolution process establishes otherwise.

## Article VII — Provenance

Original evidence and its provenance shall be preserved. A modified or substituted artifact cannot inherit the original artifact's integrity status merely by retaining its identifier or claimed hash.

## Article VIII — Enforcement

Critical governance rules must be enforced by system mechanisms, not merely by prompts, documentation, UI conventions, or model instructions.
